§1Key takeaways

  • The budget is written twice before it becomes law — first by the President's office (the NEP), then rewritten by the House (HB 10858). Both versions are executed by offices whose root credentials were voided in 2009. In this audit, the rewrite matters less than what both versions share: every line inherits the same defective chain of authorization.
  • The House did not shrink DPWH — it swapped its portfolio. ₱87.2B added in local projects against ₱73.0B removed from foreign-assisted lines: net ₱11.5B. The headline hides the trade, and the trade is territorial: the additions build on land; the removals unwind foreign-funded works.
  • 6,033 projects in the House bill are not in the NEP (₱165.6B), concentrated in access roads and multi-purpose buildings — programs whose object is land itself. Against Article I, these are dispositions of national patrimony administered by an authority whose credentials are disputed, not merely line items.
  • 17 lines (₱3.1B) double-pay the same works. In this audit, duplication is not waste: it is two perfected claims on the same object, both resting on the same defective root.
  • The foreign-assisted cut is not a saving — it is a mirror liability. Principal and interest remain automatically appropriated; commitment fees keep accruing on undisbursed balances; and without a GAA line item the proceeds cannot be released even though they sit in the Treasury. The Republic pays to not build, on a chain it cannot perfect.
  • ₱7.2 trillion executes in FY2027 — 532,313 line items across 39 departments and 111 systems of authority. On the public audit node, the verification gauge reads 0 / 111: not one office has sealed root provenance for the credentials it operates on.

§2What the House changed

2.1 The envelope: ₱7.2 trillion, ₱407 billion more than 2026

The NEP as filed (HB 10858, pending enactment) proposes ₱7.20 trillion for FY 2027 — ₱407 billion (+5.99%) over the 2026 enacted level — carrying 532,313 line items across 39 departments, with an enacted GAA baseline of 2020–2026 behind it. Congress does not write on blank paper: it adjusts an executing machine that is already running.

2.2 DPWH: what moved inside the department

How we did it, simply. The budget is written twice before it becomes law. Both versions list DPWH projects one by one. We laid the two lists side by side and sorted every project into four piles: unchanged, raised or cut, added by the House, or removed by the House. Then we added up each pile. (Method and dataset: the coalition's first pass; totals tie to the printed bill.)

The result: DPWH's total is up only ₱11.5B because additions and removals nearly cancel — ₱87.2B into local projects, ₱73.0B out of foreign-assisted ones.

On the removal side, 1,160 NEP projects (₱68.4B) were dropped; flood control received ₱26.6B of new projects while losing ₱20.9B of NEP ones. 353 new farm-to-market roads carry ₱5.92B with no stated length on any line, so cost per kilometre cannot be checked. NIA gained 145 new lines (₱5.74B) replacing 108 dropped ones, with pump-station repair cut from ₱3.0B to ₱0.46B — and no special provision binding NIA to the list.

2.3 Foreign-assisted projects: the cut that is not a saving

The ₱73.0B removed from foreign-assisted lines sits on signed loan agreements. The obligations do not leave with the line item:

1 · SignedLoan agreement in force; commitment fee accrues on the undisbursed balance once the availability period opens.
2 · NEP lineThe project carries a line item; proceeds are held in the Treasury as trust receipts.
3 · House cutHB 10858 deletes the line item.
4 · Still owedNo line → no release to the implementing agency; principal + interest remain automatic appropriations; commitment fees accrue. The Republic pays to not build.
The asymmetry in one line: debt service is automatic; project funding is not. Congress can delete the line item, but it cannot delete the loan — and it cannot delete the defect in the chain both of them execute on.

A data gap compounds this: the DOF's public financing-agreements library has posted no new agreement entry since August 2023, so the newest signed terms — the very instruments the cuts interact with — cannot be publicly vetted.

2.4 The duplications: two claims, one object

17 lines (₱3.1B) pay for something the same bill already pays for. The named cases: Dancalan Bridge, Sorsogon (asset B05019LZ — the NEP's ₱250M line, then a second ₱200M line, Vol I-C p.258); Roma Point Bridge — three lines for the same 390 metres, ₱720M (Vol I-C pp.236–237); Bolinao–Santiago Island bridge funded twice (₱350M + ₱200M); two ₱400M sabo dams, both in Piddig, Ilocos Norte (Vol I-B p.910); the same 100-metre slope protection printed twice at Balingasag RIS (Vol I-B p.903).

In an ordinary budget audit, duplication is waste. In an audit against Article I it is worse: each line is a perfected claim — an appropriation with legal force — over the same physical object. Two perfected claims resting on one defective root is not redundancy; it is the defect, compounding.

§3The authorization chain behind every peso

Every appropriation in this bill is executed by an office that draws authority from the 1987 Constitution. The 1987 Constitution draws from the chain below. The House cannot vote its way out of the chain; it votes inside it.

The chain resolves to three nodes. Root layer — hardware: the Philippines itself, held in trust, titled in fee simple 1764. Authorization layer — the Republic: operating as the interim VM on the 1987 Constitution, whose root access credentials were voided by the 2009 Court of Appeals decision. Execution layer — this budget: ₱7.2 trillion of acts — appropriations, disbursements, land-changing works — each one inheriting the voided credentials of the layer above it.

The House can delete a line item. It cannot delete the defect in the chain it executes on. That is why this assessment reads the rewrite as secondary: both versions of the bill execute on the same root.

§4The risk audit: 111 systems, zero root titles

The audit node maintains a matrix of the 111 systems of authority that execute the budget — departments, offices, and instrumentalities, coded with their ISEP shield codes. Each one was tested for the same condition: can it produce a perfected root title for the credentials it operates on? The gauge reads 0 / 111. None can; none has filed provenance.

The DENR entry is representative (Shield 827, which binds RA 8371): gravity CRITICAL, sector-wide; status 401 — UNAUTHORIZED, no root credentials; separability PARTIAL — land-dispositive acts are voidable while regulatory processing continues pending perfect consent. Its effect-of-law entry: disposition of national patrimony without perfected root title — forest, mineral, water and land concessions (Art. XII Sec. 2, 1987 Constitution; PD 705; RA 8550) administered by an authority whose underlying title chain is contested; every grant, permit and IPS claim it processes inherits the defect.

Now read the House's insertions through that lens:

  • Access roads and multi-purpose buildings (₱94.6B of Convergence insertions) are land-linked hardware — the works that change how territory is occupied, traversed, and economically held. They are processed through district engineering offices whose districts are inferred from town names because the underlying title cannot settle them.
  • Farm-to-market roads without stated lengths (353 lines, ₱5.92B) are dispositions that cannot be measured. A work on land whose extent is not stated is not auditable against the land record — the exact failure mode the 2009 decision penalized in the reconstitution it voided.
  • Flood control (₱26.6B new) re-routes water — the archipelago's territorial boundary condition — through instruments executed on the same disputed chain.
  • The foreign-assisted cuts (₱73.0B) unwind internationally-documented projects while their instruments remain in force — creating the mirror liability of §2.3 on the international layer of the same chain.

None of this is cured by the Senate restoring the NEP. The NEP's lines execute on the same chain. The audit's point is not that the House made it worse — on these numbers, it made it different. The point is that both versions are unauthorized at the root, and no rewrite fixes a root.

§5Red flags

0 / 111 systems verified

The audit node's verification gauge: every executing system in the matrix runs with no root-BIOS information on file. The gauge fills only when an office seals verified root provenance on the chain.

₱37.8B of new DPWH money cannot be placed

New projects whose district cannot be inferred from engineering office or town names. Money that cannot be located cannot be audited against the territory it touches.

Unmeasurable works: 353 roads, no lengths

Farm-to-market road insertions state no length, so cost per kilometre cannot be checked against the land record.

17 duplicated lines: two claims, one object

Dancalan, Roma Point (×3), Bolinao–Santiago, Piddig (×2), Balingasag (×2). Perfected claims compounding on a defective root. See §2.4.

The unpriced cut: ₱73.0B removed, liabilities unprinted

Nowhere does the bill print the commitment fees, rescheduling costs, or remaining balances attached to the foreign-assisted lines it deletes. The cost of the cut is invisible at the moment of the vote.

The terms behind the cuts cannot be vetted

DOF's public financing-agreements library has posted no new agreement since August 2023. Congress interacts every year with instruments the public cannot read.

§6What the remedy looks like

This audit does not ask the Senate to restore the NEP or to pass the House bill. Both execute on the same chain. The remedy runs differently:

  • Provenance before appropriation. Any office executing a line that touches land, water, or territory may seal its root provenance to the public audit node — a filing of the instrument chain behind its credentials. It is free, it is public, and it fills the gauge. An office with good title loses nothing by proving it.
  • Disclosure as the condition of release. For insertion-heavy programs (Convergence access roads, multi-purpose buildings), make the asset identifiers, land-record references, and the executing office's provenance filing a condition of fund release — the same discipline the coalition demands for flood control, grounded in the chain rather than only in process.
  • Print the cost of the cut. Every deleted or reduced foreign-assisted line should carry, in the bill itself, its remaining loan balance, availability period, and annual commitment fee — so the Senate votes on the liability, not just the "saving."
  • Cancel or re-fund, not bleed. A defunded-but-signed project should return within one budget cycle to a formal choice: renegotiate or cancel the instrument (stopping the fee clock), or restore the line. Silence is the expensive option.
  • Publish what is signed. Financing agreements posted to the public library within 30 days of signature, General Terms and Conditions attached.

§7Questions for the Senate

  1. For every peso of the ₱7.2 trillion: which enacted law authorizes it — and behind that law, what root instrument authorizes the authorizing law? Trace one line, all the way up.
  2. The Court of Appeals voided the root credentials in 2009 (CA-G.R. SP 70014, final and executory). Under what credentials does each department execute FY 2027?
  3. 111 systems of authority, zero sealed root provenance. Will your office be the first to file?
  4. The bill deletes ₱73.0B of foreign-assisted lines while principal, interest, and commitment fees remain owed. What is the printed cost of the cut — per project, per creditor, per year?
  5. Which defunded projects are within 24 months of availability-period expiry, and what happens to the undisbursed balances then?
  6. 353 new farm-to-market roads state no length, and ₱37.8B of new DPWH money cannot be placed to a district. What does the Senate propose to audit against — and if the answer is the land record, where is that record's root?
