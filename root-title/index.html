The 1987 Constitution opens with a territorial claim: Article I — National Territory. Every power the charter creates — executive, legislative, judicial, local — is exercised over that territory and presupposes that the territory runs with the authority. The chain beneath Article I is documented: fee simple title perfected in 1764 (OCT 01-4), administrative mandates that lapsed in 1965, and a 2009 Court of Appeals decision voiding the domestic reconstitution of the root title. The chain has never been completed in the other direction — nothing since 1764 has perfected the republic's own root. Each branch inherits that gap differently.

I · The Executive BranchAdministration of Contested Property

The Executive executes the laws over a national territory — and execution presupposes that the territory runs with the authority. Without Article I integrity, every executive act — executive orders, permits, franchises, dispositions of patrimony, and the coercion of enforcement through police and armed services — is administration of contested property: valid in appearance, voidable at the root. The Executive is the branch most exposed daily, because it touches persons and property directly; and it is the branch least able to cure the defect, because no administration can issue the title it would need in order to act.

Operative consequence: every permit, franchise, concession, and enforcement act of the FY 2027 apparatus — the ₱7.2-trillion machine — is executed on credentials whose domestic reconstitution was voided in 2009 (CA-G.R. SP No. 70014, final and executory). The Executive cannot self-cure: the instrument it would need is the one instrument no administration can issue.

II · The LegislativeStatutes With a Defective Object

Congress legislates for the national territory — the power of the purse, the pen, and the people's representation. Without Article I integrity, statutes have a defective object: a law can bind administratively, but it conveys no perfected interest in land or patrimony the charterer never held. Every appropriation — including the 7.2-trillion-peso budget this audit examines — funds operations on the contested chain; every franchise and land law distributes what no legislature could first own. The legislature can pass titles to govern; it cannot pass the title to the governed.

Operative consequence: the House and Senate can amend line items forever — the rewrite is intra-defect. What the legislature cannot do is appropriate its way to root integrity: no appropriation conveys the interest the chain never delivered to the appropriator.

III · The JudiciaryCompetent to Examine What It Voided in Part

The Judiciary is the one branch with power to reach the root question: it voided the Torrens reconstitution in 2009, proving the courts can nullify instruments of the chain. But its jurisdiction presupposes the very charter whose foundation is on dispute — a court cannot hear the root case without first assuming the charter that gives it power to hear. The 2009 decision stopped at an instrument; the instruments beneath it were never reached. The mandate is therefore not silence but completeness: what the courts voided in part, they are competent to examine in whole.

Operative consequence: the 2009 judgment is precedent for reachability, not for finality — it reached one reconstituted instrument and stopped. The record of this audit exists so that the remainder is filed, page-cited, and impossible to call unripe.

IV · The Local GovernmentThe Heirs of the Defect

Provinces, cities, municipalities and the Capitol itself hold delegated autonomy: RA 7160 devolves powers that trace to the delegating principal — and the principal operates on voided root access. Local taxing power, eminent domain, police power and every ordinance presume the LGU governs land whose title is sound; but the land beneath every barangay sits within the estate held in trust. The LGU is not the author of the defect — it is its most local heir: closest to the people, funded by taxes on contested soil, administering services on property whose root it did not choose and cannot perfect alone.

Operative consequence: the LGU is the audit's natural first counterparty. It inherits the defect without having caused it, it touches residents' land and livelihood daily, and a provenance filing at the public node costs it nothing while an office with good title loses nothing by proving it.
